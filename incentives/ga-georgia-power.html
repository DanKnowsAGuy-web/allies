What this utility pays today

The compressor-cycling controller and the solar thermal system with storage fit Georgia Power's custom upgrade track, which pays $0.11 per kWh on measured yearly reduction up to 2,000,000 kWh and $0.07 per kWh above that1. Preapproval isn't required, but Georgia Power highly recommends it, so a written yes from the utility comes first1. The solar unit is contested, since Georgia Power may read its rule against savings from swapping another energy source for electricity to cover solar heat2.

The refrigerant-side treatment of existing cooling equipment would go through Georgia Power's measured maintenance and recommissioning track, which pays on test-in and test-out savings at $0.11 per kWh up to 2,000,000 kWh and $0.07 per kWh above3. That fit is low confidence, and a written yes from the utility comes first here too4.

Rooftop unit replacement has a printed rebate on Georgia Power's prescriptive heating and cooling list, at $80 per Ton for an air-source heat pump package from 65,000 to under 135,000 Btu/h5. Rebates on that list share a cap of $100,000 per building per year up to 50% of equipment cost, and preapproval is recommended but not required5.

The demand charge

We don't have a confirmed Georgia Power demand charge on file for this card, so we read the rate from the customer's bill. A demand charge bills the highest kW the site pulls in a billing period, and a rooftop unit's compressor starts add to that peak. On a 10-ton rooftop unit, that peak sits right beside the price and the equipment as one of the three overspend buckets on the bill.

Demand response

The Curtailable Load Rider (CL-1) from Georgia Power is for customers who can drop at least 200 kW of demand when the company asks6. It pays monthly credits worked out in each customer's agreement, and no per-kW rate is printed6. It pays for load reduction, and it is not an equipment rebate.

Financing

Georgia C-PACE works county by county: a county or city can use Peach State C-PACE as the statewide administrator only if it adopts its own C-PACE through it, and Invest Atlanta runs the City of Atlanta's7. Named improvements include HVAC systems and building automation systems7. Today 4 of 155 service counties are confirmed: DeKalb, Douglas, Fulton and Putnam7. We don't have the statute text on file, so treat the legal details as not yet confirmed.
