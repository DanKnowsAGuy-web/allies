Utility incentives, New York

Consolidated Edison Company of New York (Con Edison)NY

As of October 9, 2026

What this utility pays today

The compressor-cycling controller on a rooftop unit, rooftop unit replacement and the all-in-one solar thermal system with storage can each go through the Con Edison commercial and industrial program, which pays $0.35 per kWh on custom electric savings once the utility approves the project1. Payments are capped at 50% of the project cost, and total incentives at $1,000,000 per account per year1. Work can't start until Con Edison inspects the site and sends a Notice to Proceed1. On the Jamaica network in Southeast Queens, the same program adds a neighborhood bonus of $2,000 per kW saved at the summer network peak once the utility approves the project, for applications made by December 31, 20261.

The refrigerant-side treatment of existing cooling equipment has no confirmed fit in the Con Edison small business program yet, so we are asking Con Edison2.

The demand charge

On SC 9 General Large, Rate I, low tension service pays $44.05 per kW of maximum demand from June through September and $34.50 per kW the rest of the year3. High tension service pays $30.41 per kW in summer and $21.17 per kW in the other months3. A demand charge bills the highest kW the site pulls in a billing period, and a 10-ton rooftop unit's compressor starts add to that peak. There is no ratchet, though a minimum monthly charge is tied to the highest demand registered in the most recent eighteen months3. That is the peak bucket of overspend; the price and the equipment are the other two.

Demand response

Con Edison Auto Dynamic Load Management pays for load relief, not for equipment4. No fixed reservation rate is published, because each participant bids its own rate per kW for the season through an open request for proposals4. Participants contract for at least 50 kW of load relief, and aggregators can bid, so a smaller site can join through one4.

Financing

C-PACE is enacted and in force in New York under the General Municipal Law, and each municipality opts in by local law5. NYCEEC administers PACE financing for New York City, and the Energy Improvement Corporation runs Open C-PACE for its member municipalities5. C-PACE is confirmed in at least 5 of 6 service counties and we confirm it for the address5. The law covers heating and cooling system upgrades and renewable energy systems, and state guidance adds thermal energy storage5.

Watch

  • October 15, 2026: commercial and industrial projects must be complete by this date to lock in 2026 incentive rates1.
  • December 31, 2026: the commercial and industrial program expires on this date, or sooner if funds run out1.

Next step
