Utility incentives, Pennsylvania

FirstEnergy Pennsylvania Electric CompanyPA

As of October 9, 2026

What this utility pays today

FirstEnergy Pennsylvania can pay for a compressor-cycling controller on a rooftop unit, a refrigerant-side treatment of existing cooling equipment and an all-in-one solar thermal system with storage through Custom Equipment and Process Improvement, at $0.04 per kWh and $250 per peak kW on the savings CLEAResult accepts, once the utility approves the project1. Pre-approval isn't required, but funds aren't reserved until it is granted, so we get the utility's written yes before the customer buys anything1. Each project is capped at the lower of 50% of total project cost and $500,000 per project, and each location at $1,000,000 per calendar year1.

Rooftop unit replacement gets up to $600 per unit as an instant discount at the distributor through FirstEnergy Instant Discounts, for a unit whose AHRI rating meets 11.8 EER and 14.9 IEER2. No application is needed, and the discount tops out at $20,000 in incentives per project2.

The demand charge

On Rate GS-Medium, the distribution charge per kW of billing demand depends on the rate district: Met-Ed $5.51, Penelec $6.99, Penn Power $4.13 and West Penn $3.46, the same in all billing months3. We read the site's district from the customer's bill. Billing demand can also be set by a share of the highest demand from earlier months3. A demand charge bills the highest kW the site pulls in a billing period, and a 10-ton rooftop unit's compressor starts add to that peak3. That is the peak bucket of overspend; the price and the equipment are the other two.

Demand response

FirstEnergy pays for load reduction, not for equipment, through the Daily Load Shifting and Peak Demand Reduction program4. The plan lists its payments only as up-to values, and FirstEnergy agreed to revise them by technology, so we are asking for current values before we quote a site4. FirstEnergy hasn't published a kW threshold to join4.

Financing

Pennsylvania's C-PACE law is in force with no statewide administrator; a county may contract a third party to run it, and the county pages mostly name Sustainable Energy Fund5. C-PACE is confirmed in at least 23 of 55 service counties, and we confirm it for the address5. The law covers heating and cooling equipment and solar thermal equipment, while controls and refrigerant additives are not named, and C-PACE is a way to finance the project, not a rebate5.

Watch
