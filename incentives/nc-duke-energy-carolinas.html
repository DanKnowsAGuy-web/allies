Utility incentives, North Carolina

Duke Energy Carolinas (North Carolina)NC

As of October 9, 2026

What this utility pays today

Rooftop unit replacement has a printed rebate in Smart $aver Business Prescriptive Rebates, Non-Lighting / HVAC (Carolinas): $151 per ton for a mid-size rooftop air conditioner at 14.8 IEER or better, and $47 per ton for a heat pump in the same size class1. No pre-approval is needed, the unit is installed and running before the application goes in, and the rebate is capped at 75% of the incremental equipment cost1.

The compressor-cycling controller and the all-in-one solar thermal system with storage may qualify in the Smart $aver Custom Incentive Program (Carolinas), which prints $0.075 per kWh and $170 per winter peak kW for HVAC controls and HVAC equipment2. A written yes from Duke comes first: the pre-approval offer letter has to arrive before any contract, purchase order or equipment order, and the payment is the lesser of that rate or 75% of incremental measure cost2.

The refrigerant-side treatment of existing cooling equipment would go through the Classic Custom track, which looks at maintenance work case by case with no printed rate, so a written yes comes first there too3.

The demand charge

On Schedule SGS Small General Service, the first 30 kW of billing demand each month carries no charge, and every kW above that costs $4.82 per kW4. A demand charge bills the highest kW the site pulls in a billing period, and a 10-ton rooftop unit's compressor starts add to that peak4. There is a ratchet: billing demand never drops below 70% of the highest 30-minute demand in the last 12 billing months, so one summer spike sets a floor under the bill for a year4.

Demand response

Duke's PowerShare load curtailment rider pays a monthly capacity credit for each kW the site agrees to curtail: $2.50/kW on the longest notice, $5.00/kW on 30 minutes' notice and $5.50/kW on 10 minutes' notice, plus $.10 per kWh for energy curtailed in an event5. The site has to contract at least 100 kW of curtailable demand, and this pays for load reduction, never for equipment5.

Financing

North Carolina's C-PACE Act, enacted in 2024 and now in force, lets any local government join a statewide C-PACE effort run by the Economic Development Partnership of North Carolina for the Department of Commerce6. Our count shows 10 of 44 service counties taking part, including Mecklenburg, Guilford, Forsyth and Durham6. Qualifying improvements are permanently affixed to the building and must use less energy to do the same job, and C-PACE finances the project rather than paying a rebate6.
