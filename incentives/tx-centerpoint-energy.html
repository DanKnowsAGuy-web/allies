What this utility pays today

Rooftop unit replacement is on the list for the CenterPoint Commercial Standard Offer Program, which pays on the verified peak kW a project takes off the bill, plus the kWh it saves1. The line has no published rate, so we are asking CenterPoint to confirm the class, DX cooling units at $317.00 per kW and $0.11 per kWh, and the buyer should apply before work starts2.

The program may consider unlisted measures case by case, but the compressor-cycling controller has no confirmed fit or class yet, and we are asking CenterPoint32. The program lists renewable technologies, but the all-in-one solar thermal system with storage has no confirmed fit either, and we are asking CenterPoint42. The newest published terms for the CenterPoint Market Transformation Programs date from 2024 and leave the refrigerant-side treatment of existing cooling equipment with no confirmed fit, so we are asking CenterPoint5.

The demand charge

On CenterPoint's Secondary Service Greater Than 10 kVA schedule, the distribution charge is $4.893378 per kVA of billing demand, all year6. A demand charge bills the highest kW the site pulls in a billing period, and a 10-ton rooftop unit's compressor starts add to that peak. There is no ratchet: billing kVA is the current month's peak6. That is the peak bucket of overspend; the price and the equipment are the other two.

Demand response

The lever here is a charge the site avoids rather than a payment: once an interval-metered site on secondary service has peaked above 700 kVA in any earlier month, the 4CP transmission charge under Rider TCRF bills it $6.177655 per kVA on the 4CP basis7. Cutting load at the 4CP peaks lowers that charge, which rewards load reduction and is not an equipment rebate7.

Financing

Texas C-PACE is run by the nonprofit Texas PACE Authority with the city or county that opts in under Local Government Code chapter 3998. C-PACE is confirmed in at least 4 of 12 service counties, Fort Bend, Galveston, Harris and Montgomery, and we confirm it for the address8. We don't have the statute text on file yet. It finances permanent improvements that cut energy use or demand, including devices that regulate temperature, and it is a way to finance the project, not a rebate8.
