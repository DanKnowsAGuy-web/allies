What this utility pays today

Rooftop unit replacement pays $294.79 per kW plus $0.095 per kWh of deemed savings through the Oncor Commercial Standard Offer Program, paid to the approved service provider, so we confirm the customer's share with the provider1. Oncor must approve the project before it starts, and incentives never go above 50% of total project cost1.

The compressor-cycling controller on a rooftop unit, the refrigerant-side treatment of existing cooling equipment and the all-in-one solar thermal system with storage can each go through the Oncor Rural Commercial Program as custom projects, and the amount is set case by case once the utility approves the project2. The statewide evaluator reviews the custom calculations before approval, so the application goes in before work starts2. For the solar thermal system, the packaged unit can also take the high-efficiency HVAC line at $0.105 per kWh on projects saving up to 500,000 kWh once the utility approves the project, though that line credits nothing for the solar assist2.

The demand charge

We don't have Oncor's demand charge on file yet, so we read it from the customer's bill. A demand charge bills the highest kW the site pulls in a billing period, and a 10-ton rooftop unit's compressor starts add to that peak. That is the peak bucket of overspend; the price and the equipment are the other two.

Demand response

Under Rider TCRF, Oncor bills transmission cost on a site's 4CP kW, so a lower 4CP kW means a lower charge, which rewards load reduction rather than equipment3. Secondary Service above 10 kW pays $6.536625 per kW on the 4CP basis, and Primary Service on a distribution line pays $6.207957 per kW on the same basis3. It applies once a site has peaked at 700 kW or more in any earlier billing month3.

Financing

Texas C-PACE is administered by the Texas PACE Authority, a nonprofit, and each city or county sets up its own program under the Local Government Code4. C-PACE is confirmed in at least 34 of 99 service counties and we confirm it for the address4. It finances permanent improvements fixed to the property that cut energy or water use or demand, including equipment that regulates temperature4. We don't have the statute text on file yet.
